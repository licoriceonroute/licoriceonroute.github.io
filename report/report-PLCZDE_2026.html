Polska–Česko–Deutschland

Bike Trip - July 11th – 16th 2026

Including Dresden - July 17/18th

Preface

For a few years now I had debated biking in Poland. Among the things that drew me to consider it were the mountains along the border with the Czech Republic and Slovakia, as well as the wild camping options in a lot of the forests there.
So when my dad retired and immediately asked me to take him on a bike trip, among the ideas I had was the border region of Poland, the Czech Republic, and Germany. In addition, I had been to Saxonian Switzerland previously and had hiked in the beautiful sandstone mountains. From the high plateaus I had looked down to the Elbe and thought about the bike path there.

In the end we decided to go to Poland and bike from to the source of the Elbe, crossing the border between the three countries multiple times and ending in Dresden for a day of sightseeing.
One big benefit of this route was the accessibility of it, we could take our loaded bikes on the trains with in both Germany and Poland. We would be camping on some nights and in a hotel once as well.

I look forward to the landscapes, the food, the architecture, and a tenting trip with my dad. The last time he tented was in 2022 for a night on the shoals with me and my brother, before that it was over a decade prior.

Day 1 | Journey East | Saturday 11.07.2026
Train: 567km | Hamburg-Görlitz
Metrics: 6.7km Bike

We arrived at the station early to get some sandwiches and to avoid being held up by the road closures for the triathlon.
The trip got off to a good start immediately as my dad fell on the stairs to the platform. Luckily he was wearing his helmet, but nevertheless the result was a deep gash in his ear that started instantly dripping blood. After some light fainting and under his insistence that he was ok, we got on the train and he recovered his strength well enough to cycle to the emergency room on arrival in Dresden.
Here we spent about four hours, getting three stitches and a fairly reasonable bill for €120. The staff was very kind and was very excited to have a Canadian in the ER, the exotic passport itself almost overshadowing the injury at hand.

We returned to Dresden-Neustadt and got on the train to Görlitz to at least make some forward progress. From the train we spied Bautzen, which seemed impressive and worth visiting.

In Görlitz we got a hotel in the city centre. After a nice dinner we took a long walk through the town and admire the architecture before it gets dark. Tomorrow morning we do the same after breakfast.

Day 2 | Re-Plan | Sunday 12.07.2026
Stage: Legnica to Wieścizowice
Bike: 7:08(4:18) | 66.56km | ↗️918m ↘️535m | 12'930 Strokes
Weather: 15-27° Overcast

After our breakfast we have some time before our train so we take another walk to see Görlitz in the sun. The town walls have been partially converted into a park, and we take full advantage.

Once at the train station we buy some lunch to take along and wait. Our train to Jelenia Góra doesn’t arrive and a staff member on the platform suggests at least taking the next RE1 to Zgorzelec as a late Polish train might rather turn around there.
On arrival in Zgorzelec we are told the train to Jelenia Góra is at least 40min late and that we would miss our connection there to Walbrzych. As we wait another train pulls up to the platform opposite. This one isn’t going to the cities we want to, but it does pass through Legnica which is about 50km north of Walbrzych. In a snap decision I buy us some tickets and we get in a few minutes before it leaves.

Our route from Legnica will be longer than planned, but at least we will start earlier and we will get there at all.
Going south out of Legnica along a park we change into our bike shorts and continue, crossing through wide open fields and small towns.



The highlight of this section is Jawor, more specifically the Church of Peace in Jawor. Built as one of three churches according to an allowance of the Treaty of Westphalia to the protestants there[1],[2], the exterior is deceptively simple, making the richly painted and decorated interior all the more stunning.



Leaving Jawor along a bike path before diving on to a forest road and starting the first real climb of the day. Our arduous climb up a frankly unpleasantly rough gravel road was sweetened somewhat by sour cherry trees which lined the way.



The next forest (Part of Krajobrazowy Chelmy Park) offered up multitudes of raspberries. But as we gobbled them up, they were already exacting their revenge. I had picked up a prickly passenger and was soon faced with a flat front tire.
Apart from the flat, that second forest was lovely, with smooth gravel roads that wove in and out of clearings lined with flowers.



After leaving the second forest, the climbing intensified and we had to re-route to avoid some forest paths made impassable by forestry works.



Skipping down a footpath to asphalt again we climb up to a castle and I have to stop and eat something, I haven’t had enough today and I can really feel it.

We decide to shorten the day a little and also detour to pass by an open grocery store.
The store is packed, looking at the map later it must be the only place open on Saturdays after 18:00 within 20km. We get some things for breakfast and lots of sugar for the next day and after one last climb we make the campsite around 20:30

A shower is in order, and we eat our pasta as the sun goes down and finish off with some cookies and chips. The much longer day than planned is over, but tomorrow will bring the real challenge.

Day 3 | Source | Monday 13.07.2026
Stage: Wieścizowice to Harrachov
Bike: 14:02(7:19) | 79.71km | ↗️2'565m ↘️2'381m | 16'435 Strokes
Weather: 15-31° Overcast to Clear

We wake up fairly early and have a breakfast of yogurt and fruit, we pack our bikes and head south into the forest.

The main attraction of the town we stayed in (Wiesciszowice) is a series of lakes and canyons. It seems to us that the town becomes a parking lot and destination on the weekend as explained by the campground owner.
Either way, now it’s devoid of people and we walk the rooted and gravel paths alone. Left to explore the rocks and ponds.
The colourful lakes are as such because of mining activity which dates back hundreds of years. We see one mine entrance, the shafts gated, but nothing else really remains.





We head up the gravel forest roads again and after a short break on a lookout we descend down a very washed out forest road and on to a field where we drop into a village. A few more climbs and descents pass by before we arrive in Karpacz.



Karpacz is a resort town, mostly for skiing but it’s packed here now as well. As we ascend out of the town, we pass people from all over Poland, and many even from as far away as the Netherlands and Denmark all enjoying the sunny weather. Interestingly in Karpacz there is a 13th century Norwegian Church that was transplanted here from Norway by the Prussians in 1842 [3] a very odd story.



After descending again we reach a road under construction, and past it are very few cars and very smooth asphalt. The first few kms of the steepest climb of the day pass almost pleasantly. The gradient ramps up however and becomes impossibly steep the closer we get to the border, with some sections well over 20%. We share the deteriorating road with hikers, pushing ourselves towards the pass.

Finally we reach the pass, and before us lies the Czech Republic. Up here there is a parking lot, a big hotel and restaurant, all accessible from the Czech side by a very nice road, much less steep and in much better condition than the Polish one.
We take in the view and make the plunge, speeding downwards around the hairpins. The descent is excellent, and near the bottom we can already hear the young Elbe, flowing into Špindleruv Mlýn and we follow.



A much needed stop at a restaurant for a Czech lunch and some Kofola (Czech Cola) gets our spirits up for the next segment. The whole day has been either ascents or descents, and the remainder is no different.

The climb begins directly behind the restaurant, but refreshed it doesn’t seem all that bad. The asphalt road winds up the side of the mountain, through the clearings where the winter crowd skis, past the immobile chairlifts we yearn to use, the road continues until it stops in a pile of logs. Here we descend again, erasing so much of the upward progress we made just to have to climb again, and higher.

The gravel track brings us down to a small ski station, where we pass and take a serpentine road, crawling higher up the switchbacks. On this climb and the last we stop for breaks frequently, I act as a domestique, handing out chocolate bars, mentos, gummy bears, and water to my big GC hope.




The road deteriorates, the asphalt splitting and revealing the intricately placed cobblestone beneath. Snaking our way from smooth patch to smooth patch we start the last switchback, pausing for a breather we admire the view. The whole Czech Republic below us, stretching as far as the hazy sky allows us to see. The final push takes us above the forest, and here we have another fine gravel track, the now alpine environment only allowing shrubs and small individual trees to grow and giving us a great view of the surrounding mountainsides looking back to the Polish border. Here large huts mark the ridge in both directions, we wonder how many hikers are holed up inside, enjoying the same view.



We cycle down towards the hiking path as far as we dare, knowing we’ll have to make it all back up, before leaving the bikes on the side of the road and continuing on foot.
One of our goals this trip, the only actual goal beyond having fun, is to see where the Elbe emerges from the earth.
We’re not there yet, first we take our detour to the Elbe waterfall, where the young Elbe streams into its valley for the first time.
I enjoy a Franzbrötchen (brought from Hamburg and carried for three days) by the banks, just like I would on the banks of a much fuller and more industrial Elbe back in Hamburg.



Near the waterfall is a hotel, huge for for being so hard to access[4], but we aren't staying here tonight.

We walk back up to the bikes and reach the path to the spring, here we push the bikes on foot the last km or so until we reach the ceremonial beginning of the waters that will flow thousands of kilometres to Hamburg and the North Sea.
There is a monument of sorts here. A depiction of the river and the coats of arms of cities on its banks. Many I know, some I’ve only heard of, the rest I will have to look up later. Still here in the dying light, atop the mountain, I can’t help but feel like I’ve succeeded in something.




Now we’re in a rush, a race against the sun, and in that we are decidedly the losers. The technical part of the descent at least is still made in twilight, but as the road improves the light fades and we are left gliding down a smooth surface next to a rocky stream we decide is probably very beautiful, a constant companion to descend alongside through the night.
We make the campsite a full hour after sundown and my dad goes for an involuntarily cold shower as I set up the tent. He’s already asleep when I return from mine.

Day 4 | Airport | Tuesday 14.07.2026
Stage: Harrachov to Bogatynia
Bike: 9:56(3:43) | 51.97km | ↗️778m ↘️1096m | 7'974 Strokes
Weather: 13-29° Overcast to Clear

We wake up late-ish and pay for the previous night before heading out.
The campground office walls are covered from rug to gable in little commemorative pennants and pieces of broken skis, testament to the many competitions and races held on the hills nearby. One catches my eye, it says Montreal 1986, I ask what it says and am told it’s for a modern pentathlon competition in Montreal, and that the text is in Polish. Who competed or how it ended up here is a mystery to the two campground workers as well.

We leave the campground and cycle to a restaurant in town for breakfast. The atmosphere in the restaurant is interesting, part market, part Café, music is softly playing off of a turntable next to a stack of records.
The food is amazing, I have a great eggs Benedict and my dad goes for the full English breakfast (happily for home, they use better sausages than English breakfast Sausages in the Czech Republic). After breakfast we need some more food for the road, having eaten most of it on the long climbs the day before.

At a supermarket we get some fruit and candy. At the adjacent butchers we get sausages (four small smoked ones, two covered in black pepper, two normal cured sausages, and one spicy chorizo) and a thick slice of smoked cheese.

We finally truly start closer to eleven, first out of the town on a short incline which turns into a long shady climb next to another stream demarcating the Polish-Czech border. It’s probably similar to the one last night. The moss covered forest glows green in the dappled light, finally giving way at the top to a long open field, dotted with houses. It’s almost a hamlet, but they’re scattered and almost isolated from each other. Cycling through, there are many families and hikers enjoying the sunny day along the path. We come across probably the biggest building of the clearing, two guys in gis emerging from inside what turns out to be a martial arts gym (it also hosts floorball and volleyball).




We dip into the forest again to descend from the high clearing, first down a bike path and then a road, smooth asphalt and hairpins take us down to another stream, this time we take a break. Setting up our chairs on the bank, watching the water wash through the rocks, eating some sausages and cheese along with our Laugengebäck, taking a dip in the cold eddies, relaxing for a while in a beautiful place and enjoying nature.



After our break we leave the forest behind break out into another town and begin the more urban part of our day.
The towns are small, the houses simple, the factories abandoned. We stop at a basilica where more tourists than expected flow through, with even a few cycle-tourists passing by.




The biggest highlight of this stretch is Frydlant Castle, the first Castle Museum in Central Europe, opening to visitors in 1801. Partly built in the baroque and then in the renaissance style, it’s very pretty and a perfect last point of interest before a classic dinner in the town square of Frydlant where even the pretty buildings can’t mask the empty storefronts.



We strike out on our last leg to find a campsite for the night. This is one of the Polish forests that can be camped in and is mostly formed of a hill built of the leftover from the vast nearby coal mine (more on that tomorrow).

After crossing the border again into Poland we hit a few steep climbs, every one marking a step higher on this gigantic slag heap.

Along the dirt roads we pass a few open spots, but decide to continue past until we reach a derelict runway in the middle of the forest.

The old airport is made up of large concrete slabs, still perfectly level and—judging by the tire marks—a favourite place to do some burnouts. The wind blows through and reminds us how bug-free it is out of the trees. That’s all it takes, we pick the western end and pitch our tent in the middle of the runway, wedging the tent-pegs between the cracks and attaching the fly to the bikes on either end.



The sun goes down behind the overcast sky, and we sit for a little on the concrete before retiring into the tent.
During the night I hear the barking of what is probably wolves, and the distant thunder of a storm, but neither finds its way into the tent.

Day 5 | Sandstone | Wednesday 15.07.2026
Stage: Bogatynia to Hřensko
Bike: 7:08(4:34) | 76.02km | ↗️724m ↘️973m | 10'812 Strokes
Weather: 16-32° Sunny

Morning on the runway is bright and quiet, the sun comes up over the eastern end and encourages us out of the tent and down off of the hill. The trails start off as gravel like yesterday, but quickly change to large concrete slabs, giving us a bump every three or so metres.

We descend down the slag heap and reach the reason for the mound in the course of the morning. First as a system of conveyor belts carrying slag, then as a power plant looming above the village we cycle through.

Having passed through the village we circumnavigate the large Turów open pit coal mine before arriving in germany and heading for a spot of breakfast in the centre of Zittau.





After breakfast we pedal west back towards the Czech Republic, and in doing so pass through an area of fairly unique architecture.
The houses here are all built in a specific way, be they from the 1500s or from 2006. The "good rooms" for entertainig and living were in the section with the wooden arches, whereas the kitchen and working space was opposite in the area built of masonry. This style of house is called Umgebindehaus [5] and also has the function of saving timber while freeing the wooden walls from the load of the upper floors.





From the houses in Germany we cross the border to the Czech Republic, and after a shady rest in the woods, we descend into the sandstone gulches of the Czeche Republic.
The route here is smooth and car-free, we even see some other cycletourists, although they all seem to be on daytrips. Still the sun beats down on us, unblocked by the small poplars that have replaced the formerly ubiquitous spruce trees.




We meet a real road and descend into the tourist town of Hřensko. Altough Karpacz was for people on vacation, Hřensko feels less like a place for people to visit and stay than a pure tourist attraction. The old charm boiled down and distilled into a facade of pretense and greed. Along the pedestrian street the stalls are filled with tat, junk quality clothes, plastic garden statues. None of it has a place here, and yet there seems to be little else around.
The hawkers on the streets and the staff in our Hotel seem bored, listless and are, without exception, Chinese. I wonder if they have come to stay and sit beside piles of plastic hats and rubber shoes, or if it's simply seasonal.

Still, Hřensko's beauty is undeniable. The tourist attration still works. Old houses in a thin, deep, valley; flanked by the sandstone cliffs.

Day 6 | River | Thursday 16.07.2026
Stage: Hřensko to Dresden
Bike: 6:10(3:22) | 60.43km | ↗️223m ↘️240m | 8'320 Strokes
Weather: 18-33° Sunny

The day begins with a breakfast which isn’t necessarily bad, but it is odd. The components are all there, just the layout and the style is amiss. Like someone had recreated a continental breakfast after having one described to them over the phone.

Everything was the cheapest thing possible. Apple/Strawberry and Apple/Cherry Jam. The bread rolls, the cheese, the juices, the salami, the cold breakfast frozen pizza, the just too soft donuts.

We crossed the street to the ferry and so left the Czech Republic by boat, continuing on the German side and on the Elberadweg (Elbe Cycling Route). This route is sometimes on both or just one bank and can switch, but travels continuously down the Elbe through Hamburg and to the North Sea. Ever since I had visited Saxonian Switzerland in 2023 I had wanted to ride at least part of it.



The route was mostly very calm and well maintained, the only traffic being other cyclists and the odd pedestrian.

Along the way we came across the Fortress of Königsstein, built on top of a high plateau overlooking the Elbe.



Further down, after crossing the Elbe again (this time by way of water-current powered ferry) into Rathen, we park the bikes and hike up to one of the most famous tourist attractions / landmarks in the area. The Basteibrücke: a bridge built high into the towers of sandstone above the Elbe, originally for a castle that has since been destroyed. But the bridge remains, and is still very impressive, as impressive are the views of the Elbe valley. Below we can see kayakers and rafters speed by, swept along by the fast current.






From Rathen we continue on the north side until Pirna and switch again, stopping only for a break of sausages by the banks, and in Pirna for some Sodas and a look at the old town.

Pirna is the last stop before Dresden, from here the path becomes busier, with commuters and people out for a day from the city. We pass under the Blaues Wunder, a famous steel bridge, considered a technical marvel at the time. On the opposite bank large villas rise above on the hillside, as we get closer to Dresden they are replaced by three grand palaces and then more modest Appartement blocks.

Dresden itself is to be best explored tomorrow. We stop at the Frauenkirche as the formal goal of our ride, and continue to the rental, coincidentally we look across the street directly at the ER we visited last Saturday. What a nice way to close the loop.

Post Trip 1+2 and Thoughts | Dresden | Friday+Saturday 17+18.07.2026
Train: 480km | Dresden-Hamburg
Metrics: 29.4km Bike

A day in Dresden is well spent doing touristy things, the Zwinger, the palace museum learinng more about the saxon kings and thier favourite collecting hobbies, puttering around the city and the riverside. A dinner in the stained glass dome of an old cigarette factory wraps it up nicely (look up Yenidze in Dresden for reference, it's not as weird as it sounds).



The last day we spend biking into the hills south of Dresden, exploring the large gardens, and taking a visit to the Glass Factory of VW.

That evening we board our train back to Hamburg, returning to the other end of the Elbe; a place where enormous ships carry the world’s cargo on brackish tidal waters.
Now we know where it all starts, and how far the water tumbling down the waterfall has to go. I enjoy the new reflection I see in the river I’ve lived next to for 11 years now: a little part of the Czech Republic, of Saxonian Switzerland, of Dresden, and of home.
